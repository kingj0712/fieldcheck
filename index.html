This world

Plankton blooms on its own. Grazer fish school together, eat plankton, and spend energy as they swim. Feed enough and — with a close, compatible mate — they breed; starve and they fade.

Every fish carries seven heritable traits: speed, size, sense, colour, plus gape (how big a mouth — high gape lets a fish eat other, smaller fish), boldness (how long it grazes before fleeing), and schooling. Offspring recombine both parents' traits with small mutations.

Because fish only breed with genetically similar partners, lineages that drift apart stop interbreeding and become distinct species — you can watch them split in the colour spectrum.

One tier is carnivorous: predatory fish (warm-coloured, larger) hunt smaller fish and fry. High gape trades against grazing, so it's a real gamble — predators boom when prey is thick and starve when it thins. Some are here from the start, the tide brings more, and any lineage can drift toward hunting on its own.

Colour is functional: fish whose hue matches the water are harder to spot, so camouflage is selected for — against the pull of each species' own colour.

Hunters are the apex tier: larger, faster, always carnivorous. Every death — grazed, hunted, or starved — sinks as detritus that decays back into plankton, so the whole pool is one closed, self-feeding cycle. Booms and crashes, no ending.

Tools

1 Feed — sprinkle plankton.
2 Stir — push the water; things scatter.
3 Fish — release a fresh random fish (a possible new lineage).
4 Hunter — release an apex hunter. Use sparingly.
